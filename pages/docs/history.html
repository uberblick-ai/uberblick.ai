Uberblick / Docs / Core concepts

Document history

Your colleagues edit a page in the afternoon, an agent works through it overnight, and by morning a number has moved and a sentence says something slightly different. Changes like these are easy to miss and expensive to find late. So every page keeps its history,1 and you can look back at any earlier version and see exactly what changed.

No change slips by

A knowledge base that people and agents write together only works if you can trust it. Trust comes from seeing what happened: who changed this paragraph, when, and what it said before. We wanted that to take seconds, not an archaeology session, so you can read a change, decide whether it's right, and move on.

Nothing to save

Nobody should have to remember to save a version. Revisions are made on their own while people and agents edit, and edits that belong together are grouped into one step. The history reads like the story of a page, not a stream of keystrokes.

Familiar ways to read a change

We took our cues from the diff views developers live in every day, in their editors and on GitHub: the earlier version beside the later one, with everything that didn't change folded out of the way. Not everyone reads changes that way, and for them an inline view, closer to tracked changes in a word processor, is on its way.

Anchors in a long history

Some versions matter more than others: the one the team agreed on, the one a release shipped with. Give that version a name and it becomes an anchor you can always come back to. Soon you will also read the history from anchor to anchor, with all the small edits in between folded into one step.

JSON gets its own comparison

A lot of our own discussions happen around data: the shape of a schema, an API answer, a config an agent runs with. Comparing that as lines of text hides what actually changed, so we compare it by its structure. A new key reads as one addition, a changed value as one change, and reformatting doesn't count. It felt right to give the thing we argue about most a view of its own. See JSON trees.

Agents look back too

The history is open to agents as well. An agent can check what changed on a page since it last worked on it, or quote what a page said when a decision was made, instead of guessing from the current text.

1 History lives on the hub, so it covers workspaces you share through one. A workspace that stays on your laptop keeps no history.
