Is Uberblick a good fit?

Uberblick is a narrow tool. It holds the product intent, the decisions and the numbers behind one or more codebases, close to where the coding agents and the engineers work. Product managers define and adjust that intent in it and enrich it with data from other systems, Mixpanel or New Relic for instance. To be fair: it is early, and it is built by a small team with no ambition for profit. We are amazed how fast we can build the system itself with the raw power of Uberblick and ub-agents, but you should enjoy being a bit of a beta tester.

If you start with a fresh idea, or feel the pain of too many outdated Markdown files, and you like that you can still influence where the product goes, feel free to use it. Below is when it fits, when it does not, and how it compares.

A good fit when

  • A team of a few people to a few dozen builds one product with coding agents, and wants the agents to read the plan and the decisions before they build. Not hoped for: enforced, by the MCP server the agents talk to.
  • The documents are about that product: intent, design notes, decisions, reports with their numbers in them, the flows and screens behind a feature.
  • Engineers write most of it with their agents; the product people read, comment and decide in the browser.
  • The team is at home in a terminal, installs with Homebrew, writes Markdown and keeps delivery on GitHub.
  • It already has an agent it trusts and would rather connect it than get another assistant.
  • It wants the documents on its own machines: every computer holds the full workspace and works offline, and sharing means one Docker container the team can reach.

Not a good fit when

  • The content is company-wide: onboarding, HR, sales, meeting notes for everyone. Notion, Confluence or Google Docs do this better. Uberblick focuses on small product and tech focused teams.
  • You need someone to host it. Nobody hosts Uberblick for you; sharing needs the hub you run. But it is just a Docker file: Run a hub.
  • You want tasks. There are none and there will be none. Uberblick is not a task manager; delivery lives in the tracker.
  • Your issues live in Jira, Linear or GitLab. Uberblick links to GitHub and only GitHub today.
  • Your team is on Windows. We are on macOS and Linux so far; Windows might work, and if you need it, we can make it happen. Phone support will come; today a phone can read and comment in the browser.
  • You need enterprise permissions. A workspace has members and admins, signed in with GitHub. No SSO, no per-page rights, no audit log.
  • You want "ask the wiki" built in. Uberblick has no AI of its own, and that is deliberate: quite frankly, MCP in your favourite AI desktop app has come a long way, and every built-in approach we have tried feels outdated next to it. If a question box inside the product is what you want, Notion, Confluence and Outline ship one.
  • You use it as a solo developer. It might work: Uberblick was started by a solo developer and it helped. But Obsidian or Trilium are probably better suited to one person's notes.

Some of the above may be added later. We are focused on getting the core of the app right and will expand on what is requested and what feels right. File an issue and request what you need.
